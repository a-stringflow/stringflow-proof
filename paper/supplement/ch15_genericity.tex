\section{Genericity ledger for $(a,b)$}

The string-flow framework is written for the accelerated map
\begin{equation*}
  T_{a,b}(x)=\frac{ax+b}{2^{v_2(ax+b)}}.
\end{equation*}
This chapter records which parts of the framework are generic in
$a$, which parts are specific to $(a,b)=(5,1)$, and which parts are
not claimed.  The ledger does not prove any new statement for other
parameters.

\subsection{Status labels}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Generic}: the statement holds for the indicated family
  of parameters with the same proof shape.
\item \textbf{Parameter-dependent}: the statement shape is generic,
  but its constants or hypotheses depend on $a$ and must be
  re-derived.
\item \textbf{$(5,1)$-specific}: the statement is proved only for
  $(a,b)=(5,1)$.
\item \textbf{Not claimed}: the statement is not asserted in this
  paper for other parameters.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What the generic layer gives}

The generic layer is the exact word ledger:
\begin{equation*}
  2^{\wordWeight(w)}\,x_L=a^Lx_0+\wordA(w),
\end{equation*}
the PMI identity
\begin{equation*}
  \sum_{j=0}^{L-1}2^{-m_j}
    =\frac{a\,\wordA(w)}{a^L},
  \qquad m_j=j\log_2 a-\wordWeight_j,
\end{equation*}
and the cycle equation.  These identities are independent of the
starting value and of the arithmetic of $a$ beyond the word being
valid.  They are the part of the framework that can be reused without
new proof.

\subsection{What is not transferred}

Everything after the word ledger is tied to the concrete arithmetic:
the finite prefix, the segment exclusions, the modular exclusions,
the CRT residue, and the decisive window thresholds.  In particular,
the constant $13$ and the thresholds $+10$, $+11$, $+12$ are proved
for $(5,1)$; this paper does not claim they survive for $a=3$.

\subsection{The first obstruction question}

The paper does not assert which lemma would be the first one that
fails for $a=3$.  Within the $(5,1)$ proof, the earliest layer where
the parameter enters arithmetically is the segment-size layer
($g<5^{j-1}/4$ in the $d=1$ exclusion), followed by the rise-step
residue rules and the decisive-window thresholds.  A concrete answer
for $a=3$ would require re-developing the segment exclusions and the
window arithmetic; that development is not part of this paper and is
therefore marked as not claimed.
